\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Recovering the 6-DoF pose of an object---its orientation in $\SOthree$ and its
position in $\mathbb{R}^3$, jointly an element of $\SEthree$---from a single image
is a core problem in computer vision and robotics. In many settings the pose is not
uniquely determined by the image. A textureless cube looks identical under its $24$
rotational symmetries and a cylinder under a continuous one-parameter family; depth
is ambiguous under perspective; and self-occlusion or a missing distinguishing
feature can make otherwise distinct poses indistinguishable. A method that returns a
single pose discards this structure; a method that returns a \emph{distribution}
over the pose group preserves it, and lets downstream consumers---grasp planners,
trackers, sensor-fusion pipelines---reason about the ambiguity
explicitly~\cite{murphy2021ipdf,haugaard2023spyropose}. The difficulty is
\emph{normalization}: a usable distribution must integrate to one over the group,
and for the expressive non-parametric models this is exactly the expensive part,
on $\SOthree$ and more so on $\SEthree$.

The most expressive way to represent such a distribution is non-parametrically.
Implicit-PDF (IPDF)~\cite{murphy2021ipdf} does so with an implicit function: a
network maps an image embedding and a candidate rotation to an unnormalized
score, and the distribution is recovered by normalizing these scores over an
equivolumetric grid of $\SOthree$. This is maximally flexible and trains from a
single ground-truth rotation per image, but it inherits two costs from the grid.
First, obtaining a \emph{normalized} density requires evaluating the network at
every grid point---roughly $2.4$M rotations at the resolution used for
evaluation---so a faithful distribution is expensive and its fidelity is capped
by the grid spacing. Second, during training the normalizer is only
\emph{approximated}, by querying a random or uniform coverage of $\SOthree$
together with the ground truth. SpyroPose~\cite{haugaard2023spyropose} makes the
grid hierarchical and replaces the uniform coverage with importance sampling,
reducing the number of evaluations by orders of magnitude and extending the idea
to $\SEthree$; it trains an energy per resolution with a contrastive objective
whose partition function is an importance-sampling estimate, and at inference
expands the top-$k$ cells level by level and renormalizes their children. Its
inference distribution is properly normalized, but it is obtained by a
multi-query sparse search and its fine-level density depends on the expansion
width $k$.

\paragraph{Our approach.}
We ask whether the normalizer can be removed from the problem entirely rather
than approximated more cheaply. Our observation is simple: the same
Hopf--HEALPix construction that IPDF and SpyroPose use for its equivolumetric
property is also \emph{strictly hierarchical}---doubling the grid resolution
splits every cell into exactly eight disjoint children, forming a tree. On a
tree, a normalized distribution over the leaves can be written as a product of
per-node categoricals (a tree-structured softmax~\cite{morin2005hierarchical}): a categorical over
the $72$ cells at the coarsest level, and at every node a categorical over its
$8$ children. Because each factor is a softmax, the product is an exactly
normalized probability \emph{by construction}---at every resolution, with no grid
evaluation and no sampled partition function. The density at a rotation $R$ is
\begin{equation}
  p(R) \;=\; P\big(\text{finest cell of } R\big)\cdot \frac{N}{\Vso},
\end{equation}
and $\log P(\text{cell})$ is the sum of the log-categoricals along the single
root-to-leaf path that contains $R$. Evaluating the likelihood of any rotation
therefore costs $O(L)$ node scorings for a grid of $N=72\cdot 8^{L}$ cells,
rather than the $O(N)$ of a retrieval grid.

This argument never used anything special about rotations: it holds for \emph{any}
pose group with a strict hierarchical equivolumetric partition. We therefore state
the method as a general framework (\cref{sec:framework}) and instantiate it twice:
on $\SOthree$ with the Hopf--HEALPix tree above, and on $\SEthree$ by taking the
Cartesian product of that rotation tree with a bounded equivolumetric translation
octree (\cref{sec:se3}), under which a joint cell factorizes into a rotation path
and a translation path and the exact normalization and $O(L)$ likelihood carry over
unchanged.

We instantiate the tree with a compact head on top of a vision
transformer~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit}. A set of learned queries cross-attends to
the patch tokens to produce the coarsest categorical; a single shared child
scorer, conditioned on each child's geometry relative to its parent, produces the
$8$-way categorical at every node. The output layer is zero-initialized, so the
model starts from the uniform distribution and the training log-likelihood starts
at the exact uniform floor $-\ln \Vso$. Training is maximum likelihood of the
single rendered ground-truth rotation---no symmetry annotations, matching the
IPDF protocol.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \emph{general framework} for pose-distribution estimation that is
  \emph{exactly normalized by construction}: a tree-structured softmax over any
  strict hierarchical equivolumetric partition of a pose group. It needs neither a
  retrieval grid nor a sampled partition function, the exact log-likelihood of any
  pose is read off in $O(L)$ from a single path, and a uniform predictor provably
  scores $-\ln V_G$---a data-free self-test (\cref{sec:framework}).
  \item Two instantiations: $\SOthree$ via the Hopf--HEALPix tree ($8$ children per
  node), and $\SEthree$ via the Cartesian product of that tree with a bounded
  equivolumetric translation octree ($64$ joint children per node), which keeps the
  exact normalization and $O(L)$ likelihood and adds only a detector-sized
  translation box (\cref{sec:se3}).
  \item A lightweight cross-attention head on a frozen-init vision transformer that
  realizes the construction, trained by single-ground-truth maximum likelihood, with
  an $O(bL)$ beam-search MAP decoder.
  \item An evaluation on SYMSOL~I under both the distribution and pose metrics, with
  analysis and ablations (\cref{sec:experiments}): a single joint model reaches
  $7.71$ average log-likelihood, above the strongest RGB-only baseline ($7.33$,
  trained per shape), while reading the exact likelihood of any rotation from a
  single $7$-node path rather than scoring a million-cell grid; its one weak case is
  the high-order icosahedral object, whose $60$ equivalent modes must be covered at
  once. The $\SEthree$ instance is exactly normalized by the same construction, which
  we verify numerically; we define a single protocol that carries it, together with
  SYMSOL~II and the real-image BOP datasets T-LESS and YCB-Video, across both pose
  groups (\cref{sec:se3results}).
\end{itemize}
