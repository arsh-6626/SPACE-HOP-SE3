\subsection{Equivolumetric Partition Trees and Density}
\label{sec:trees}

The Hopf--HEALPix construction of Sec.~\ref{sec:hopf} is applied recursively to
obtain a rooted \emph{partition tree} $\mathcal{T}$ over $SO(3)$. The root level
($\ell=0$) is the base partition at $N_{\mathrm{side}}=1$, comprising
$N(0)=12\times 6=72$ equal-volume cells. Each cell at level $\ell$ is split into
\emph{eight} children by simultaneously (i) subdividing its HEALPix base pixel
into its four NESTED descendants and (ii) halving its fiber interval. Hence the
fiber resolution $K=6N_{\mathrm{side}}$ doubles at every level, and the number of
cells at depth $\ell$ is
\[
N(\ell)=72\cdot 8^{\ell},\qquad N_{\mathrm{side}}=2^{\ell}.
\]
Because each split preserves equal area on $S^2$ and equal length on $S^1$, all
cells at a given level share the same Haar volume
\[
\mu\big(C^{(\ell)}\big)=\frac{\pi^2}{N(\ell)},
\]
and each parent is the disjoint union of its eight equal-volume children. The
partition is therefore nested and equivolumetric at every resolution.

A leaf at depth $L$ is identified with a root-to-leaf \emph{path}
$\mathbf{c}=(c_0,c_1,\dots,c_L)$, where $c_0\in\{1,\dots,72\}$ selects a root cell
and each $c_\ell\in\{1,\dots,8\}$ selects a child. The model factorizes the cell
probability autoregressively over depth,
\[
P_\theta(\mathbf{c}\mid I)=
p_\theta(c_0\mid I)\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}
p_\theta\!\big(c_\ell \mid c_{<\ell}, I\big),
\]
where $p_\theta(c_0\mid I)$ is a categorical over the $72$ root cells and each
$p_\theta(c_\ell\mid c_{<\ell},I)$ is a categorical over the $8$ children. Since
every conditional sums to one, summing $P_\theta$ over all leaves telescopes to
$1$: the distribution over cells is \emph{exactly normalized by construction},
with no partition function to estimate.

The continuous density is piecewise constant on the partition. For a rotation
$R$ falling in leaf cell $C(R)$,
\[
p_\theta(R\mid I)=\frac{P_\theta\big(\mathbf{c}(R)\mid I\big)}{\mu\big(C(R)\big)}
=\frac{N(L)}{\pi^2}\,P_\theta\big(\mathbf{c}(R)\mid I\big),
\]
which integrates to one against the Haar measure. Equal cell volumes are what
make this exact: the softmax outputs map directly to density with no per-cell
Jacobian, and uniform conditionals yield the uniform density $p_\theta\equiv
1/\pi^2$. Evaluating the likelihood of an arbitrary $R$ requires only locating
its cell (HEALPix \texttt{ang2pix} in NESTED order plus the fiber bin) and
accumulating the $L{+}1$ log-conditionals along its path, an $O(L)$ operation.

\subsection{SE(3) Octrees}
\label{sec:se3}

We extend the construction from $SO(3)$ to $SE(3)=SO(3)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^3$ by
pairing the rotation tree with a translation \emph{octree} over a bounded domain.
Translation is restricted to an axis-aligned box $\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^3$
anchored to the detection (Sec.~\ref{sec:symmetry}); the octree root is
$\mathcal{B}$, and each node is split into its $8$ octants by bisecting along
each axis. A translation cell at depth $L_t$ has volume
$V_{\mathcal{B}}/8^{L_t}$, and the octant index is encoded by one bit per axis
(a Morton code).

The joint $SE(3)$ tree is the product of the two hierarchies: at each joint level
a node has $8\times 8=64$ children, indexed as
$\text{child}=8\,c^{\mathrm{rot}}+c^{\mathrm{trans}}$, i.e. a rotation child
paired with a translation octant. The joint cell probability again factorizes
autoregressively and telescopes to one, so
\[
p_\theta(R,\mathbf{t}\mid I)=
\frac{P_\theta(\mathbf{c}\mid I)}
     {\mu_{\mathrm{rot}}\,\mu_{\mathrm{trans}}},
\quad
\mu_{\mathrm{rot}}=\frac{\pi^2}{N_{\mathrm{rot}}(L)},\;
\mu_{\mathrm{trans}}=\frac{V_{\mathcal{B}}}{8^{L_t}},
\]
is an exactly normalized density over $SE(3)$ with respect to the product of the
rotational Haar measure and Lebesgue measure on $\mathcal{B}$. The uniform floor
is $p_\theta\equiv 1/(\pi^2 V_{\mathcal{B}})$. The read-out translation is
recovered from the leaf octree cell as $\mathbf{t}=\hat{\mathbf{t}}+A\,
\mathbf{g}$, where $\hat{\mathbf{t}}$ is the detection-based translation anchor,
$A$ scales the normalized box to metric units, and $\mathbf{g}$ is the cell
center in normalized coordinates. This joint tree inherits the exact
normalization, multimodality, and $O(L)$ likelihood evaluation of the rotation
tree, now over the full $6$-DoF pose space.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification}
\label{sec:uq}

Because NeST emits a full normalized density rather than a point estimate,
uncertainty is read directly from the predicted distribution. We summarize it in
three complementary ways. First, the \emph{predictive entropy}
$H[p_\theta(\cdot\mid I)]=-\sum_{\mathbf{c}}P_\theta(\mathbf{c}\mid I)\log
p_\theta(\mathbf{c}\mid I)$, computed over the leaf partition, measures overall
concentration. Second, the \emph{top-mode mass} and the angular (and
translational) dispersion of the highest-probability leaves quantify how tightly
the belief is localized. Third, genuine ambiguity---whether from object symmetry
or from an uninformative view---appears as \emph{multiple} high-mass leaves,
which we extract as the top-$k$ modes by beam search over the tree
(Sec.~\ref{sec:training}).

Our working hypothesis is that this spread is \emph{calibrated}: the model should
be diffuse exactly when it is likely to be wrong, so that predictive entropy
correlates with pose error and a small oracle set of modes captures the correct
pose under symmetry. We evaluate this via (i) the rank correlation between
predictive uncertainty and geodesic/translational error and (ii) oracle-$@k$
accuracy as a function of the number of retained modes.

\subsection{Architecture and Conditioning}
\label{sec:arch}

The conditioning image is encoded by a ViT-B/16 backbone (AugReg2
initialization, fine-tuned at learning rate $10^{-5}$), producing a set of patch
tokens $X\in\mathbb{R}^{196\times 768}$ that serve as keys and values for every
node of the tree. The tree categoricals are produced by two cross-attention
heads. The \emph{root head} applies $72$ learned queries that cross-attend to $X$
and emit the root logits $p_\theta(c_0\mid I)$. The \emph{child head}, whose
weights are \emph{shared across all levels}, forms its query from the geometry of
the candidate transition: the child offset $[\Delta_{\mathrm{rot}}(3),
\Delta_{\mathrm{trans}}(3)]$ and the parent code $[R(9),\mathbf{g}(3)]$ are passed
through a Fourier positional encoding and a two-layer projection
($126\!\to\!768\!\to\!768$, GELU), added to a per-level embedding, and
cross-attended to $X$ to emit the $8$-way ($SO(3)$) or $64$-way ($SE(3)$) child
logits $p_\theta(c_\ell\mid c_{<\ell},I)$. Both heads are two-layer, eight-head
cross-attention blocks with a \emph{zero-initialized output projection}, so the
network begins exactly at the uniform density floor and departs from it only as
training proceeds. Decoding is autoregressive over depth: the chosen cell's code
conditions the query at the next level.

\subsection{Symmetry and Augmentation}
\label{sec:symmetry}

\paragraph{In-plane roll augmentation.} The Hopf fiber corresponds to rotation
about the camera optical axis (in-plane roll). To prevent the model from
memorizing a canonical roll---a failure mode we observe on symmetric
shapes---we augment each training sample by a random in-plane rotation
$\psi\sim\mathcal{U}[0,2\pi)$, applied jointly to the image and the target pose.
This forces the fiber categoricals to be learned from appearance rather than
dataset bias.

\paragraph{Translation and depth normalization.} Rather than regressing metric
translation directly, we express it relative to the detection: the anchor
$\hat{\mathbf{t}}$ is obtained from the bounding box and known object diameter,
and $A$ normalizes the translation box to the object scale, so the octree target
$\mathbf{g}$ is depth- and scale-normalized. We additionally apply depth
($z$-scale) augmentation to decorrelate apparent object size from true depth.

\paragraph{Object symmetry.} We do not enumerate symmetric pose labels during
training. Each sample is supervised with its \emph{single} annotated pose
(Sec.~\ref{sec:training}); the exactly-normalized, multimodal density is free to
place mass on all symmetry-equivalent orientations, which it learns to do from
the data. Symmetry is accounted for only at evaluation, through the standard
symmetry-aware (minimum-over-equivalents) error.

\subsection{Training Objective and Inference}
\label{sec:training}

\paragraph{Objective.} Training minimizes the mean negative log-likelihood of the
ground-truth pose. For each pair $(I_i,T_i)$ we locate the leaf cell containing
$T_i$, giving its path $\mathbf{c}^\star_i$, and minimize
\[
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left[\log p_\theta(c^\star_{0,i}\mid I_i)
+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\log p_\theta\!\big(c^\star_{\ell,i}\mid c^\star_{<\ell,i},I_i\big)\right].
\]
Because the tree-structured softmax is exactly normalized, this NLL is a proper
scoring rule evaluated in closed form: there is no partition-function estimation,
no contrastive negatives, and no Monte-Carlo normalization.

\paragraph{Inference.} The model supports several queries at $O(L)$ cost each.
(i) \emph{Likelihood}: the density at any pose is obtained by descending to its
cell and accumulating log-conditionals. (ii) \emph{MAP / mode finding}: we decode
the most probable leaf by greedy descent or beam search with width $B$, keeping
the top-$B$ partial paths per level; the pose is read out as the cell center,
$R$ from the Hopf--HEALPix center and $\mathbf{t}=\hat{\mathbf{t}}+A\mathbf{g}$.
(iii) \emph{Multimodal output}: the top-$k$ leaves give the $k$ most probable
pose hypotheses. We report average log-likelihood on a fine evaluation grid for
$SO(3)$ benchmarks and BOP average recall from the MAP pose for $SE(3)$
benchmarks.
